Part 2 p5.Polar Template by Joanna

1. Drag mouse to change color
and use magiscope/eraser effect,
2. Click any key to invert rainbow
and black & white colors on the
polar and grid ellipses,
3. Click mouse just to randomly
change the visual.

Discuss your original drawing.

My original drawing makes use of both
polar and grid ellipses, random rotation
from static anchor points, random HSB
(HSB stands for hue, saturation, and
brightness) and monochrome color changes.

Then, share how does your drawing using
p5.Polar differ from your original version.

My second drawing incorporates an increase
of p5.Polar library operations/commands as
I originally made my first drawing using a
single polar ellipse command. I added both
polarLine and polarHeptagon commands to the
background shape (the original polarEllipse)
in my drawing. I also increased the y
coordinate and decreased the height of the
grid ellipses.

How did your work transform throughout this process?

I played around a lot with color controls to try
and make an invert effect where the polar ellipses,
lines, and heptagons in the background held color
when the grid ellipses in the foregroung were void
of them and vice versa. I also discovered the eraser
and drag effect just through play with the fill(200,200);
as the first value offers a background color match, while
the second value acts as a high transparency... only
erasing what's in the foreground and revealing what is
in the background more clearly.

What does using for loops and/or p5.Polar afford you to
experiment with that a ‘brute force’ method might not
account for?

Using for loops and the p5.Polar effects allows us the
means to be able to play with rapid and efficient
multiples of shapes and colors, in a seemingly organic
matter. The brute force method acts as a clunkier,
more labor intensive alternative.
